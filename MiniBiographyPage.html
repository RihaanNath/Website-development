Mini Biography:DR APJ Abdul Kalam

Dr APJ Abdul Kalam was an eminent aerospace scientist and the 11th President of India (2002–2007). Known as the "Missile Man of India", he played a pivotal role in the nation's civilian space program, military missile development, and nuclear tests, dedicating his life entirely to science, education, and public service.

Early Life

Humble Beginnings: Born on October 15, 1931, into a Tamil Muslim family; his father was a boat owner and imam.

Early Hardwork: Distributed newspapers during childhood to help support his family financially.

Education:Studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, and later specialized in aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.

Important Achievements

SLV-III Project: Served as Project Director for India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle which successfully deployed the Rohini satellite in 1980.

Missile Development: Led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), creating key strategic missiles like Agni and Prithvi.

Pokhran-II: Played a vital organizational and technical role in the 1998 nuclear tests, establishing India as a nuclear-armed state.

The People's President:Served as India’s President from 2002 to 2007, transforming Rashtrapati Bhavan into an open, inclusive space for citizens and students.

: Awarded India's highest civilian honor, the Bharat Ratna, in 1997.
